Scoop joints

A concave fillet where a floating control meets the surface it opened, so the panel visibly belongs to the tab that summoned it. Everything below runs in the app's own theme.css tokens at true size — the Inspector panels are 320 px because that is what .inspector is at ≥840 px.

01 — the shape

Three parts, one background

The neck is a plain rounded rectangle. The quirk is entirely in the two boxes flanking its base: each is fillet × fillet, painted in the panel's own colour, with a single concave corner. Drag the dials — every specimen further down reads the same three variables. 


        

      

      
        Anatomy. Neck 62px / fillet 26px / rise 30px — exaggerated so the curve reads.
        Shipping values are roughly a third of that.
      
    


    

      

        The two ways to cut that corner are not equivalent, and the difference decides how much of a
        fallback you owe Safari:
      

      
/* Path 1 — real box geometry. Border, outline and box-shadow
   all follow the concave curve. Chromium 139+ only. */
.joint__fillet--l {
  border-top-left-radius: var(--joint-fillet);
  corner-shape: scoop;
}

/* Path 2 — masks the paint only. Any shadow on the fillet is
   clipped away with it, so the panel's elevation stops at the neck. */
.joint__fillet--l {
  mask: radial-gradient(circle var(--joint-fillet) at 0 0,
                              #0000 99%, #000 100%);
}

      

        Toggle safari fallback above and watch the specimens. On a flat panel the two
        are indistinguishable. The moment the panel carries elevation, path 2 loses the shadow
        across the joint — which is exactly where the eye is looking.
      

    

  


  
  

    

      02 — the ask
      
The Inspector, jointed

        it says this surface is what that tab opened. Both tabs stay visible and reachable
        while you read a fragment's properties — the invariant the two-tab rewrite was for.

What this actually fixes

      

        The underline indicator is correct and unmemorable. The joint is the same information given
        geometrically: you cannot look at the panel without seeing which tab owns it, and the
        pills read as floating above the surface rather than being a strip carved out of its top.
        The collapsed edge tab keeps working unchanged — it is outside the dock.
      

      
What it costs

      

        The neck's position is a measurement, not a layout. It needs a ResizeObserver on the
        dock plus a recalculation on tab change, on font-size change, and on panel resize — three inputs,
        each of which silently misaligns the neck by a few pixels if you miss it, and a misaligned neck
        reads as a rendering bug rather than a style.

          Prototype B — the grown tool dock. Click Draw, Sign or Redact. The options bar
          emerges from the tool that owns it instead of floating free below.
        

        

          
B — the drawbar grows out of its tool

          

            Today the drawbar is a separate floating surface: it appears when a drawing tool is active but
            has nothing tying it to which tool. Joining it to the pressed button is Material's FAB-menu
            move, and it answers a real question — "these colours belong to Draw, not to Redact." The neck
            here travels between six positions instead of two, which makes the tracking problem six times
            more visible. It also inherits the existing horizontal-scroll behaviour of .drawbar,
            and a neck pointing at a button that has scrolled out of view is the failure mode to design for.

          Prototype C — clamp versus anchor. Click any button in the bar, including the
          ones at the far edges. The panel clamps inside the frame; the tail stays on the button.
        

        

          
C — the pointer that survives being clamped

          

            This is the one worth prototyping before anything else, because it is where the pattern usually
            breaks. TooltipHost already needed a layout-effect clamp or edge controls drew tooltips
            half off-screen; ColorField already had to portal to document.body because a
            transformed ancestor captured its position: fixed. Add a tail and you get a
            second constraint on the same element: clamp the panel, but keep the pointer on the anchor.
            Solve it once, in one component, and everything else in the app inherits it — ColorField,
            TooltipHost, the overflow menu, the Inspector dock.

D — squircle everything, and nothing else

          

            The cheapest quirk on this page: one declaration in theme.css, no JS, no measurement,
            no accessibility surface, and a graceful nothing-happens on Safari. It shifts the whole app's

          Prototype E — the perforated seam. Punched holes standing in for the
          plain 1px rule between DOC_GROUPS.
        

        

          
E — a tear-off seam between groups

          

            A punched perforation is a document-shop idiom rather than a generic
            software one — it belongs to this app in a way a gradient never would. It also encodes something
            true: those three groups are a real taxonomy in DOC_GROUPS, not a visual rhythm. The
            risk is legibility at small sizes, and it is not theoretical: the first build of this specimen
            punched 3.5px holes out of a 1px rule and read as an ordinary dashed border — the idiom
            evaporates and you are left with a divider that merely looks slightly wrong. It needs a hole
            diameter floor of roughly 7px, which in turn sets a minimum height for the seam.
          

        

      


    

  


  
  

    

      04 — the spec you already adopted
      
Half of this is catching up, not inventing

      

        theme.css opens with "Material 3 Expressive design tokens." But the file predates
        what Expressive actually shipped: five new components, a 35-shape library with morphing
        transitions, and — the big one — physics-based springs replacing duration-plus-easing as
        the primary motion model. The app has the colour roles and the shape scale. It does not
        have the motion system or the morph.
      

    


    

      

        

          

            
            
          

          

            Corners are the state indicator. The focused tab rounds up, the unfocused one rounds
            down — no underline, no neck, no measurement.

F — the morph that needs no measurement

      

        Material's own guidance for menus is that the focused submenu's corners become more rounded
        while the unfocused one becomes less rounded. That is the same information the scoop joint
        carries — "this one is active, and this surface is its" — delivered entirely through a property
        that needs no anchor tracking, no ResizeObserver, and no second rendering path.
        It is strictly cheaper than Prototype A and strictly more on-spec. Prototype A is more
        distinctive; F is more defensible. They also compose: a jointed dock whose active pill rounds up.
      

      
The motion problem is the real gap

      

        --ease-spring is commented in the source as a "springy overshoot approximation," and
        that is exactly what it is — a cubic-bezier can overshoot once and stop. A spring overshoots and
        settles, and the settle is the part that reads as physical. CSS linear()
        expresses that properly, works in every current browser, and is a drop-in replacement for the
        easing token. Expressive also defines two schemes — standard and expressive — so components can
        opt into visible bounce rather than every transition inheriting it. Today the app has one token
        doing both jobs.
      

      
The component mapping

      

        Expressive's five new components map onto surfaces this app already has, which makes the
        overhaul a rename-and-adopt exercise rather than a redesign: the tool dock is a
        button group, the drawbar is a floating toolbar (explicitly
        preferred over a bottom app bar), Prototype B is a FAB menu, and the
        Download button with its ⋯ sibling is a split button. Adopting the
        vocabulary is free; adopting the motion scheme is a token swap; adopting the morph is Prototype F.
      

    

  


  
  

    

      05 — the boundary
      
Where it stops

    

    

      

        
page frame — correct

        
page frame — scooped

      

      The document is not a surface in your design system.
    

    

      
Never on the page frame, the thumbnails, or anything that renders PDF content

      

        The user is looking at their file. Every pixel inside .page is a promise about what
        will come out of exporter.ts, and a scooped corner on paper breaks that promise
        twice: it implies a shape the exported PDF does not have, and it makes the app's chrome and the
        user's content share a visual language when the entire point is that they are different things.
        The same rule already governs the colour sampler — it declines rather than guessing. Chrome can
        be quirky. Content is reportage.

Five things that will bite

      

        

          

            
Safari and Firefox get the fallback, and the fallback is the majority path

            

              corner-shape is Chromium 139+ only, around 67% of users, with no public timeline
              from Mozilla or Apple. Two rendering paths means two things to keep in sync, and the mask path
              cannot carry the panel's shadow across the joint. Decide up front whether the joint is
              load-bearing information or enhancement — if the underline indicator comes out, it is
              load-bearing, and then the fallback has to say the same thing some other way.
            

          

        

        

          

            
The 360px budget is already spent

            

              shell.spec.ts fails if the app bar cannot fit itself at 360px, and
              .appbar__download only survives because it carries flex: none. A lifted
              dock adds --joint-rise of vertical chrome to whatever it sits on. On the phone
              layout the Inspector is a bottom sheet, not a column, so the joint would have to point
              down from the sheet's top edge or not exist there at all — and "not exist there" means
              the pattern is desktop-only, which is a different design decision than it first appears.
            

          

        

        

          

            
Measurement means new dependency bugs, in a codebase that has had them

            

              runOcr was [pdf] and captured a stale autosaveOn;
              PageView is memo'd, so its props have to be referentially stable. A neck position
              derived from a ref plus a resize observer plus the active tab is exactly that class of value.
              Every prototype marked "needs measurement" above is an invitation to the same bug.
            

          

        

        

          

            
The joint asserts a relationship, so it has to be a real one

            

              The Inspector's tabs already carry role="tablist", aria-controls and
              roving tabIndex — good, keep all of it. The neck and fillets are decorative and get
              aria-hidden. But a shape that promises an association to sighted users while the
              markup says otherwise is worse than the underline it replaced, and the drawbar has no such
              markup today.
            

          

        

        

          

            
One shape, one meaning — the icon rule again

            

              icons.spec.ts exists because RotateCw once meant rotate a page, restore
              a session, save one, and reset a style. A scoop joint that means "this panel belongs to that
              control" in the Inspector and also just decorates a card is the same failure with geometry
              instead of glyphs. It needs the same discipline and, ideally, the same kind of spec.
            

          

        

      

    


    

      
Recommended order

      

        Ship the token set and corner-shape: squircle first — it is one afternoon, it is
        reversible, and it tells you whether the softer geometry actually suits a document tool before
        anyone writes a ResizeObserver. Then build the anchored-tail component once, for
        TooltipHost and ColorField, because it is the hard part and everything else
        reuses it. The Inspector dock lands third, on top of a solved anchor. The drawbar is last, and only
        if the first three have not already spent the appetite.
      

    

  


  

    
What shipped from this page

    shipped
    
The joint (A), rebuilt. The selected Inspector tab is joined to the panel by two concave
    fillets — but hung off the button's own ::before/::after rather than a
    floating dock, so there is no rect to measure and no ResizeObserver. That removed the
    single biggest cost on this page. Masked, not corner-shaped, because it is the only
    thing saying which tab owns the panel and therefore has to render off Chromium.

    shipped
    
Squircles (D) across chrome as one @supports block, and never on a page, a
    canvas or a thumbnail.

    shipped
    
Shape-as-state (F) replacing the underline indicator, and --ease-spring
    rewritten as a real linear() spring with a second no-ring scheme beside it.

    not built
    
The anchored tail (C) and the grown tool dock (B). Both still need runtime
    measurement, and neither had a problem urgent enough to justify it once the Inspector turned out
    not to need one.

    not built
    
The perforated seam (E). The weakest of the five: it decorates a boundary the group
    headings already state.
